01
The Firm

About the name,
and the practice.

Hold Alt and type 30. A keyboard returns ▲ — the oldest notation in economics for a quantity that has moved.

That is the whole of the work. A tax cut moves a general fund. A plant closure moves a county payroll. A development moves a city revenue base for thirty years.

Every question a client brings us is finally a question about how much moved, in which direction, and for whom.

Alt-30 is an economic consulting firm producing the impact studies, fiscal analyses, revenue forecasts, and expert testimony that public and private decision-makers rely on when a policy, a project, or a budget is on the table.

The firm maintains its own records of appropriations, tax filings, and employment across fifty states and more than five hundred municipalities, back to fiscal year 1967, because measuring a change requires knowing what preceded it.

A PhD economist leads each engagement and presents the findings. We do not subcontract the analysis, and we do not send someone else to defend it.

Alt-30 began in Arizona and keeps unusual depth in that market. The method is identical in every state we cover, which is what lets one finding be compared against another.

We do not lobby, poll, or advise on message. We produce the number, disclose how it was derived, and defend it in the room.

02
How We Work

Four stages,
one standard of proof.

An engagement is scoped and priced before work begins. What varies is the question; what does not vary is that every assumption is stated, every input is traceable, and the economist who built the model is the one who presents it.

01 / Define
Frame the Question
We establish what decision the analysis has to support, what would change the answer, and what standard of evidence the room will demand. Scope, deliverable, and fee are fixed here.
02 / Measure
Build the Model
Inputs are drawn from primary filings and never carried forward from an earlier study without re-verification. Multipliers and elasticities are chosen on the record and disclosed in the report.
03 / Deliver
Present the Finding
Findings arrive formatted for the venue that will receive them: a committee, a council, a board, or a newsroom. The economist who built the model takes the questions.
04 / Maintain
Keep It Current
Most studies are filed and immediately begin to age. Ours can be maintained as a living portal that updates as new filings post, so the finding is current the next time it is needed.

05
Coverage & Markets

Public finance is local.
The method is not.

Every state writes its own budget, levies its own taxes, and shares revenue with its municipalities on its own formula. We hold the record for each and build to one methodology, which is what allows a finding in one jurisdiction to be set against a finding in another.

Arizona — Home Market
Where the firm began and where our depth is greatest: every legislative district, all fifteen counties, and the ninety-one incorporated municipalities, with standing relationships at the statehouse and in town halls.
West & Southwest
Secondary focus, where shared-revenue structures and growth dynamics closely resemble Arizona’s and our comparison sets are strongest.
National Coverage
All fifty states and more than five hundred municipalities carried in the longitudinal record, back to fiscal year 1967, and maintained continuously rather than assembled per engagement.
Federal Data Integration
State and local records are cross-referenced against Census, Bureau of Labor Statistics, and federal fiscal releases, so a local finding can be placed in a national context on the same page.

The Opportunity

What an engagement
actually buys.

Economic consulting is bought at the moment a decision has to be defended. The value is not the report; it is walking into the room already knowing what the strongest objection is and having the answer in the document.

Analysis that survives cross-examination. Assumptions, multipliers, and elasticities appear in the report rather than in a file we keep. A skeptic can disagree with an assumption and see exactly what changing it would do — which is why our findings are cited rather than disputed.

An economist, not an account team. The person who builds the model writes the report and presents it. There is no handoff between the analysis and its defense, and no one in the room who cannot answer a question about method.

Findings that do not expire on delivery. A conventional study is accurate on the day it is filed and decays from there. Delivered as a living portal, an Alt-30 analysis updates as new filings post, which matters when a bill returns in the next session.

Capability without headcount. A retained relationship gives a firm, caucus, or town the analytical function of a research department at a fraction of the cost of building one — and without carrying an analyst between sessions.

Independence you can hand to anyone. Because the firm holds no lobbying registrations and takes no position on legislation, our work can be brought into a client meeting, a committee hearing, or a newsroom without creating a conflict for whoever carries it.

Depth no national shop maintains. Most research firms model states. We model jurisdictions — the district, the county, the town — because that is the level at which a policy is actually felt and at which an argument is actually won.

Scope precedes price. We define the question, the deliverable, and the fee before any analysis begins. Clients do not receive an invoice shaped by how long the work turned out to take.

A first engagement is usually small. Most relationships begin with a single brief on one jurisdiction or one bill, which is enough for a client to judge the standard of the work before committing further.

Retainers follow the calendar. Session-length and annual relationships are structured around the legislative and budget cycles a client actually operates on, with rapid-response capacity during session.

The method does not change with the client. We are engaged by organizations on opposite sides of the same question, and the analysis is built the same way for each.
