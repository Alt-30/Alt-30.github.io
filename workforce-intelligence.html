What We Monitor

The labor market moves locally,
and it moves first.

National employment figures arrive monthly and describe a country. The decision in front of a staffing firm, an employer, or an economic-development office concerns a metro, a county, an occupation, and a quarter — and by the time the local picture reaches a news story, the opening it described has usually closed.

Workforce Intelligence is the standing function that watches your labor market so no one on your team has to.

We track the series that matter, resolve them to your geography, and report what changed and what it implies. When something moves that warrants a closer look, the closer look is already scoped.

01
Employment & UnemploymentPayroll employment, unemployment rates, and claims activity for the metros, counties, and states you operate in, reported on the federal release cycle and compared against your prior period and your peers.
02
WagesWage levels and wage growth by occupation and industry, so a compensation decision or a bid rate rests on the current local figure rather than a national average.
03
Occupations & Labor ForceOccupational demand, labor-force participation, and the supply of specific skills in a region — the difference between a market that is tight and one that is merely loud.
04
Industry Shifts & DemographicsWhich industries are adding or shedding work, how the population of a region is changing, and where the geographic differences are widening. The structural picture beneath the monthly number.
