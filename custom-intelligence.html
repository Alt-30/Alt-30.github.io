Application  —  Custom Intelligence

Need to understand something your team doesn’t have time to investigate? We’ll research it.

Custom Intelligence is a scoped research engagement: a defined question, a fixed fee, and a deliverable built for the decision it has to support. It is where the deeper analytical work sits — the studies that take weeks rather than hours, and that an internal team would never get to.

The Situation

Every organization has a question
no one inside has time to answer.

It is usually a good question. Whether a market is worth entering. What a project would actually contribute to a region. Whether a policy change will cost more than its sponsors say. What a competitor's move implies. The people qualified to answer it are consumed by the work that pays this quarter, and the question waits.

Custom Intelligence exists so it does not have to.

We frame the question with you, fix the scope and the fee, do the work, and deliver a finding that will hold up in front of whoever has to act on it.

01
Market Expansion & Competitor ResearchWhether a region, a segment, or a market is worth the investment: demand, workforce, cost conditions, and who is already there, assembled into a recommendation rather than a data dump.
02
Economic Impact & Workforce StudiesWhat a project, an employer, or a program contributes to an economy — output, earnings, employment, direct and indirect — and what the local labor market can actually supply to it.
03
Policy Impact ResearchWhat a proposed law, rule, or budget change would cost, whom it would reach, and what the record says about comparable changes elsewhere. Prepared to survive a committee, a client meeting, or a newsroom.
04
Dashboards & Executive BriefsA maintained view of the question for the leadership team that needs to keep watching it, or a brief written for the executive who has to decide this week.
How a Project Runs

Scope first.

Every Custom Intelligence engagement begins by agreeing on the question, the deliverable, and the price. No invoice is shaped by how long the work turned out to take, and the analyst who framed the question is the one who answers it.

Who Brings Us These Questions

Organizations that need the answer
more than they need the headcount.

Employers weighing a location or an expansion. Economic-development groups reporting to a board. Government relations firms that need an impact study behind a position, and associations whose members expect an answer with a source. Companies selling into government that need to know whether a program is actually funded. Campaigns and caucuses held to a claim about a district. Each has a specific question, a deadline, and no one free to investigate it properly.
