Practice  —  Corporate Procurement & Health IT

Whether a bid is winnable is usually a budget question.

Selling to government is a fiscal timing problem presented as a sales problem. Alt-30 advises vendors on budget flows, agency reporting cycles, and spend patterns across the states they pursue — which solicitations are genuinely funded, which will slip, and where tax exposure moves once a jurisdiction comes under fiscal stress.

The Situation

Most losing bids were decided
in the budget, months earlier.

A capture team reads the solicitation. The vendor that wins has generally read the appropriation behind it: how the line was funded, when the agency must obligate it, and what recent reporting cycles suggest about whether the money survives to award.

That record is public, and almost no one reads it.

Tracking appropriations from enactment through obligation is the work Alt-30 was built to do, which is why we can tell a client whether a pursuit is real before the proposal budget is committed.

01
Budget-Flow AnalysisWe follow appropriations from enactment through agency obligation in every state we cover, establishing whether the program behind a solicitation is stable, swept, or contracting.
02
Reporting CyclesAgency reporting cadence indicates when procurement windows open. We map those cycles — legislative budget review, quarterly reporting, fiscal year-end — so that a bid arrives when the agency can act on it.
03
Spend ForecastingForward projections by agency and program area, grounded in five decades of appropriations history, so that pipeline planning rests on the record rather than on market rumor.
04
Tax ExposureContinuous monitoring of the state and municipal tax movements that reach multi-state operators, including the budget shortfalls that reliably precede rate action.
